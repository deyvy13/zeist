<Callout type="success" title="Quick answer">
Yes. Civil 3D creates every section view in a group at once with the Create Multiple Section Views wizard: you pick the station range, the offset range, the elevation range, the bands and whether the views are laid out for sheets. What it doesn't do on its own is apply your firm's full standard (ranges, labels, hatching and tables) on every alignment, or re-sort the views when sample lines are added. A plugin automates that, and at Zeist we build it to order.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Glossary: the terms you'll read, in plain words">
- **Section view** — The drawing of one cross section, with its grid, elevations and labels.
- **Section view group** — The views created together, which Civil 3D lays out as a block.
- **Group plot style** — The rule that arranges the views in rows or columns and sets their spacing.
- **Elevation range** — The height each view shows: automatic or fixed.
- **Bands** — The data rows under each view, such as offsets and elevations.
- **Production and draft** — Views laid out for sheets, or placed loose in model space.
- **Plugin** — A program installed inside Civil 3D that adds its own buttons to the top toolbar.

Written for roadway designers, drafters and BIM coordinators.
</Callout>

## Hundreds of views, one at a time

On a roadway project, each sample line produces a section view, and a project can have hundreds. Creating them is the easy part. The work is getting the right height, no overlaps, your labels and hatching, and sheet-ready layouts. And redoing it after every alignment change.

Civil 3D automates more than most people use. This guide walks through what's built in, then what a plugin automates.

<Roadmap />

<TopicCard step="1" title="1. Every view depends on a sample line" time="~4 min">

A section view draws what a sample line cuts: the ground, the corridor and its surfaces. So:

- **No sample lines, no views.** The sample line group comes first; we cover it in [automated Civil 3D sample lines](/en/blog/civil-3d-sample-lines).
- **Anything that isn't a source of the group won't show in the view.**
- **Delete a sample line and its section views are deleted too.**
- **If the corridor or the ground changes, the sections update**, because they read the model.

Views are created one at a time (Create Section View) or all at once (Create Multiple Section Views), on the Home tab, Profile & Section Views panel.

</TopicCard>

<TopicCard step="2" title="2. The Create Multiple Section Views wizard, page by page" time="~7 min">

| Page | What you decide |
|---|---|
| **General** | The alignment, sample line group, station range, name, style and layer |
| **Section Placement** | Production, laid out from a sheet template, or Draft, in a grid in model space |
| **Offset Range** | Automatic, from the sample line swath widths, or a fixed width |
| **Elevation Range** | Automatic, or a fixed height that follows a section, such as existing ground |
| **Section Display Options** | Which sources are drawn, and with which style and labels |
| **Data Bands** | The data rows under each view |
| **Section View Tables** | Area or volume tables per section; only available once materials are computed |

What changes the result most is the **height**. With an automatic range, each view takes the height of what it cuts, and one odd section can produce huge views. With a fixed height that follows the ground, every view comes out the same size and centered on what matters.

</TopicCard>

<TopicCard step="3" title="3. Section views on top of each other: placement and the group plot style" time="~5 min">

**In production**, views are laid out from the sheet template and its viewport, at the chosen scale. If they don't fit, the problem is usually the template or the scale.

**In draft**, the **group plot style** lays them out, on its Array tab:

- **Rows or columns:** whether views fill across or upward, and from which corner.
- **Cell size:** uniform for all, sized to the largest view, or uniform per row or column, which packs them tighter.
- **Spacing:** the gap between columns and between rows.

After changing the style, update the group layout. Bands count too: a taller band makes every cell larger.

<Callout type="tip" title="If the views stack on top of each other">
Check three things, in this order: the elevation range, in case one odd section enlarged every cell; the spacing in the group plot style; and, in production, that the sheet template matches the chosen scale.
</Callout>

</TopicCard>

<TopicCard step="4" title="4. Labels, cut and fill hatching, and projected objects" time="~6 min">

**Labels.** Views carry offset and elevation labels, slope labels and corridor edge labels. They're defined in the styles and can be changed for the whole group.

**Cut and fill hatching.** It isn't drawn by hand: it comes from **Compute Materials**, using quantity takeoff criteria. Each material has a shape style that the view shows with its hatch. If the colors come out swapped or cut shows zero, check the surface order in the criteria.

**Tables per section.** With materials computed, the wizard can add a table to each view with cut and fill areas and volumes.

**Projected objects.** You can project feature lines, survey figures, 3D polylines, COGO points and other objects into the views, across a whole group at once. It's how you show a wall, a ditch or a control point in every section.

</TopicCard>

<TopicCard step="5" title="5. Adding views, updating and sending them to sheets" time="~5 min">

**When you add sample lines**, their views don't join the existing group on their own: create the new views and use **Move to Section View Group**. The Update command doesn't re-sort them.

**Section sheets** are created on the Output tab with Create Section Sheets, as long as the views were created in production. If you add views later, the sheets aren't regenerated automatically.

**The title block** on each sheet comes from the template. Filling in its data on every sheet is a separate job: we cover it in [title blocks filled automatically](/en/blog/revit-autocad-title-block-automation).

</TopicCard>

<TopicCard step="6" title="6. What the wizard doesn't do on its own" time="~5 min">

The wizard handles one group with one configuration. On real projects:

- **Many alignments.** A subdivision with 30 streets means running the same wizard 30 times, with the risk that one comes out with a different style or height.
- **The full standard.** Elevation range, bands, labels, hatching and tables, all per your firm's or the agency's configuration.
- **After a change.** New sample lines, views to move into the group and sheets to rebuild.
- **Labels at specific points.** Elevations at the centerline, the edges of pavement or the toe of slope, in every view.
- **Sheet order.** Numbering, the station range of each sheet in the title block, and the sheet index.

<Callout type="info" title="Signs it's worth automating">
Every submittal involves arranging views by hand. Your projects have many alignments. Views change height or style depending on who made them. Or section sheets get rebuilt after every review comment.
</Callout>

</TopicCard>
